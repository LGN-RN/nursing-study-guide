Unit 1 — Nursing and the Health Care Environment (Ch 1–5 · Q1–5)
Q1Ch 1 · Nursing Today

Which characteristic BEST distinguishes nursing as a profession rather than an occupation?

  • A.Nurses carry out tasks assigned by the physician
    ✗ Task performance describes occupational work; professions are defined by autonomy and specialized knowledge, not task-following.
  • B.Nursing has a specialized body of knowledge, code of ethics, and professional autonomy
    ✓ Correct. Professions are defined by specialized knowledge, self-regulation, a code of ethics, accountability to the public, and autonomous practice.
  • C.Nurses must be licensed by the state to practice
    ✗ Licensure is a legal requirement shared by many skilled trades; it is necessary but not sufficient to define a profession.
  • D.Nurses follow hospital policies and procedures at all times
    ✗ Following institutional policies is an employment expectation, not a defining characteristic of professional status.
Q2Ch 2 · Health Care Delivery

Which service represents TERTIARY level health care?

  • A.Annual flu vaccinations at a community clinic
    ✗ Flu vaccination is primary prevention — preventing disease before it occurs.
  • B.Blood pressure screening at a health fair
    ✗ Screening detects existing conditions early — that is secondary prevention, not tertiary.
  • C.Cardiac rehabilitation following open-heart surgery
    ✓ Correct. Tertiary care focuses on restoring function and reducing disability after illness or injury. Cardiac rehab is the classic example.
  • D.Prenatal education classes for expectant mothers
    ✗ Prenatal education promotes healthy outcomes and prevents complications — primary prevention.
Q3Ch 3 · Community-Based Nursing

Which action is an example of PRIMARY prevention?

  • A.Teaching a diabetic patient daily foot inspection
    ✗ Foot care in a diabetic patient prevents complications of an existing disease — that is tertiary prevention.
  • B.Administering childhood vaccines at a school clinic
    ✓ Correct. Vaccination prevents disease before it occurs — the definition of primary prevention.
  • C.Providing wound care to a post-surgical patient at home
    ✗ Wound care treats an existing condition — this is direct treatment, not prevention.
  • D.Conducting mammography screening for early cancer detection
    ✗ Mammography detects existing disease early — that is secondary prevention.
Q4Ch 4 · Theoretical Foundations

Using Maslow's hierarchy, a patient reports loneliness, has not eaten in 24 hrs with blood glucose 58 mg/dL, and is anxious about job loss. Which need is addressed FIRST?

  • A.Social belonging — the patient feels lonely and isolated
    ✗ Love/belonging is a mid-level need; physiological threats must be resolved first.
  • B.Physiological — blood glucose 58 mg/dL represents hypoglycemia
    ✓ Correct. Physiological survival needs form Maslow's base. Hypoglycemia is an immediate threat to life and is addressed first.
  • C.Safety and security — anxiety about job loss
    ✗ Safety is the second tier; it cannot be addressed before physiological needs are stabilized.
  • D.Esteem — the patient's self-worth is threatened
    ✗ Esteem is near the top of the hierarchy — far below the physiological base.
Q5Ch 5 · Evidence-Based Practice

Which resource represents the HIGHEST level of evidence for guiding nursing practice?

  • A.A single case report published in a nursing journal
    ✗ Case reports are the lowest level of evidence; they describe one patient and cannot be generalized.
  • B.Expert opinion from a wound care clinical nurse specialist
    ✗ Expert opinion is considered weak evidence due to potential bias and subjectivity.
  • C.A systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials
    ✓ Correct. Systematic reviews synthesize multiple high-quality RCTs and represent the highest level of the evidence hierarchy.
  • D.A clinical practice guideline from a hospital policy committee
    ✗ Guidelines vary in quality; those based on expert consensus (rather than systematic research) rank lower than systematic reviews.

Q7Ch 7 · Caring in Nursing

A nurse is with a terminally ill patient who is frightened and alone. Which action BEST reflects Watson's Theory of Human Caring?

  • A.Completing all documentation efficiently before family arrives
    ✗ Task efficiency reflects a biomedical model; Watson's theory centers on being fully present with the patient, not task completion.
  • B.Sitting with the patient, holding their hand, and inviting them to share their fears
    ✓ Correct. Watson's transpersonal caring moment is defined by full presence, emotional engagement, and honoring the patient's human experience.

Unit 3 — Clinical Judgment in Nursing Practice (Ch 15–21 · Q12–17)
Q12Ch 15 · Critical Thinking

A nurse uses SBAR to communicate about a deteriorating patient. What does the "A" represent?

  • A.Assessment — the nurse's clinical interpretation of the data
    ✓ Correct. SBAR = Situation, Background, Assessment (nurse's synthesis of what is happening clinically), Recommendation.
  • B.Action — interventions the nurse has already implemented
    ✗ Actions taken may be mentioned in Background or Situation, but "Action" is not a formal SBAR component.
  • C.Alert — a critical notification that the patient is unstable
    ✗ "Alert" is not a component of SBAR; it describes tone, not a formal step in the framework.
  • D.Acknowledgment — confirmation of receipt of the communication
    ✗ Acknowledgment may follow communication but is not part of the SBAR acronym itself.
Q13Ch 16 · Nursing Assessment

A patient says "I feel dizzy when I stand up." The nurse measures blood pressure lying and standing. How is each piece of data classified?

  • A.Both are subjective data
    ✗ Blood pressure is measured and quantified by the nurse — it is objective data.
  • B.The patient's report is objective; the BP readings are subjective
    ✗ This reverses the definitions. A patient's verbal report is subjective; measured values are objective.
  • C.The patient's report is subjective; the BP readings are objective
    ✓ Correct. Subjective = what the patient reports (only they can describe it). Objective = measurable data collected by the nurse through examination.
  • D.Both are objective because the nurse collected them both
    ✗ Who collects the data does not determine its classification; it is the nature of the data (measurable vs. perceived) that matters.
Q14Ch 17 · Nursing Diagnosis

A patient with COPD has SpO2 87% and uses accessory muscles. Which is a correctly written NANDA nursing diagnosis?

  • A."Respiratory failure related to COPD"
    ✗ "Respiratory failure" is a medical diagnosis. Nursing diagnoses address human responses, not medical conditions.
  • B."Patient needs supplemental oxygen therapy"
    ✗ This is a nursing order/intervention, not a diagnostic statement.
  • C."Impaired gas exchange related to alveolar hypoventilation as evidenced by SpO2 87% and accessory muscle use"
    ✓ Correct. Three-part NANDA format: [Problem] related to [Etiology] as evidenced by [Defining characteristics].
  • D."COPD causing low oxygen saturation and labored breathing"
    ✗ This is a descriptive sentence linking a medical condition to symptoms — not a standardized NANDA diagnostic statement.
Q15Ch 18 · Planning

Which patient goal is correctly written using SMART criteria?

  • A."Patient will improve mobility before discharge."
    ✗ "Improve" is vague (not measurable); "before discharge" lacks specificity. Neither is SMART.
  • B."Nurse will assist patient with ambulation three times daily."
    ✗ Goals must be patient-centered (subject = patient), not nurse-centered.
  • C."Patient will ambulate 50 feet in the hallway with one-person assist within 24 hours of surgery."
    ✓ Correct. Specific (50 ft, hallway, assist level), measurable, attainable, relevant, and time-bound (24 hours).
  • D."Patient will walk better each day."
    ✗ "Better" and "each day" are vague; not measurable or time-specific per SMART criteria.

A post-op patient has: acute pain 7/10, impaired mobility, risk for infection, and ineffective airway clearance (SpO2 91%). Which diagnosis is addressed FIRST?

  • C.Risk for infection — prevention is always a priority

  • D.Ineffective airway clearance — SpO2 91% is a life-threatening concern

Unit 4 — Professional Standards in Nursing Practice (Ch 22–26 · Q18–22)
Q18Ch 22 · Ethics

A nurse suspects a colleague is diverting narcotics after observing them wasting without a witness. What is the nurse's ETHICAL obligation?

  • A.Confront the colleague privately and offer support resources
    ✗ Private confrontation is not sufficient; it allows diversion to continue and does not protect patients.
  • B.Report the behavior to the charge nurse or appropriate authority per facility policy
    ✓ Correct. The ANA Code of Ethics obligates nurses to safeguard patients and report unsafe or unethical practice through proper channels.
  • C.Document the observation in the patient's medical record
    ✗ The patient's chart is not the appropriate channel for reporting staff misconduct.
  • D.Wait and observe again before reporting to be certain
    ✗ Waiting for confirmation allows ongoing diversion and patient harm; reasonable suspicion is sufficient to report.
Q19Ch 23 · Legal Implications

A nurse administers double the prescribed dose; the patient experiences no harm. Which legal concept BEST applies?

  • A.Assault — the patient was threatened
    ✗ Assault involves a credible threat of harmful contact; an accidental dosing error does not constitute assault.
  • B.Battery — medication given without proper consent
    ✗ Battery is intentional harmful or offensive contact; this was an unintentional error, not intentional harm.
  • C.Negligence — the nurse failed to meet the standard of care
    ✓ Correct. Negligence is the failure to act as a reasonably prudent nurse would. Doubling the dose is a breach of duty regardless of whether harm occurred.
  • D.No liability — no harm occurred, so there is no issue
    ✗ The absence of harm may limit malpractice damages, but the breach of standard of care (negligence) still occurred and must be reported.
Q20Ch 24 · Communication

A patient is crying and says "I feel like no one understands what I'm going through." Which response is MOST therapeutic?

  • A."You shouldn't feel that way — your care team really cares about you."
    ✗ "You shouldn't feel that way" dismisses and invalidates the patient's emotional experience — a non-therapeutic response.
  • B."Tell me more about what you're feeling right now."
    ✓ Correct. Open-ended exploration validates feelings and invites the patient to elaborate — a core therapeutic communication technique.
  • C."Everything is going to be okay. Try not to worry."
    ✗ False reassurance minimizes the patient's concerns and closes down therapeutic communication.
  • D."Have you been able to talk to your family about how you feel?"
    ✗ Redirecting to family deflects attention away from the patient's expressed need, avoiding rather than addressing the feeling.
Q21Ch 25 · Patient Education

A nurse finishes teaching a patient insulin self-injection. Which action BEST evaluates whether learning occurred?

  • A.Ask the patient if they have any remaining questions
    ✗ Asking about questions evaluates confusion but not actual skill acquisition.
  • B.Provide written step-by-step instructions for the patient to take home
    ✗ Written materials support but do not replace confirmation of learning through demonstration.
  • C.Ask the patient to demonstrate the injection technique (return demonstration)
    ✓ Correct. Return demonstration (teach-back) is the gold standard for evaluating psychomotor skill learning.
  • D.Document in the chart that teaching was completed
    ✗ Documentation should reflect the outcome of evaluation, not substitute for it — charting without evaluating learning is inadequate.
Q22Ch 26 · Informatics & Documentation

A nurse sees a colleague accessing the EHR for a celebrity patient out of curiosity, with no care relationship. This violates which principle?

  • A.The patient's right to informed consent
    ✗ Informed consent relates to medical procedures and treatment decisions, not health record privacy.
  • B.HIPAA — unauthorized access to protected health information
    ✓ Correct. HIPAA prohibits accessing patient records without a treatment-related need, regardless of the patient's public status. This is a federal violation.
  • C.The patient's right to refuse treatment
    ✗ Right to refuse treatment is a patient autonomy right regarding care decisions, not information privacy.

Unit 5 — Foundations for Nursing Practice (Ch 27–32 · Q23–30)
Q23Ch 27 · Patient Safety

An elderly confused patient is at high fall risk. Which intervention is the HIGHEST priority?

  • A.Apply bilateral wrist restraints to keep the patient in bed
    ✗ Restraints are a last resort, require a physician order, and can increase agitation and injury risk — never the first intervention.
  • B.Keep the bed in the highest position for easy staff access
    ✗ Beds must be in the LOWEST position to minimize injury if the patient falls.
  • C.Place call light within reach and instruct patient to call before getting up
    ✓ Correct. Ensuring the patient can call for help is the priority least-restrictive intervention for fall prevention.
  • D.Place a "High Fall Risk" armband and sign on the door only
    ✗ Signage communicates risk to staff but provides no direct protection to the patient.
Q24Ch 27 · Patient Safety

A fire breaks out on the unit. In what ORDER should the nurse use the RACE mnemonic?

  • A.Extinguish → Rescue → Alarm → Contain
    ✗ Attempting to extinguish before rescuing patients puts lives at risk; rescue always comes first.
  • B.Rescue → Alarm → Contain → Extinguish/Evacuate
    ✓ Correct. RACE: Rescue patients in immediate danger → Alarm (pull fire alarm/call 911) → Contain (close doors) → Extinguish if safe, or Evacuate.
  • C.Alarm → Rescue → Extinguish → Contain
    ✗ Alarming before rescuing delays rescue of patients in immediate danger; rescue is the first priority.
  • D.Contain → Alarm → Rescue → Extinguish
    ✗ Containing before rescuing and alarming delays both emergency response and patient safety.
Q25Ch 28 · Infection Prevention

Before inserting a urinary catheter using sterile technique, which action is MOST important to prevent HAI?

  • A.Don sterile gown and mask before entering the room
    ✗ A gown and mask are not required for routine urinary catheter insertion; hand hygiene precedes all procedures.
  • B.Perform hand hygiene before donning sterile gloves
    ✓ Correct. Hand hygiene is the single most important measure to prevent HAI and must be performed before gloving for every procedure.
  • C.Open the sterile field carefully without touching the sterile zone
    ✗ Maintaining a sterile field is important but does not replace performing hand hygiene prior to the procedure.
  • D.Apply clean gloves first, then change to sterile gloves
    ✗ Applying clean gloves before sterile gloves contaminates the outer sterile gloves; hand hygiene precedes all gloving.

Q27Ch 29 · Vital Signs

A patient has BP 148/94 mmHg on two separate visits. How should this be classified per ACC/AHA guidelines?

  • A.Normal — within acceptable limits for an adult
    ✗ Normal BP is <120/<80 mmHg; 148/94 significantly exceeds this.
  • B.Elevated blood pressure (prehypertension)
    ✗ Elevated BP is defined as systolic 120–129/<80; 148/94 exceeds Stage 1 on both measures.
  • C.Stage 2 hypertension
    ✓ Correct. ACC/AHA: Stage 2 HTN = systolic ≥140 OR diastolic ≥90. At 148/94, both thresholds are met.
  • D.Hypertensive crisis requiring emergency treatment
    ✗ Hypertensive crisis requires systolic >180 AND/OR diastolic >120. 148/94 does not meet this threshold.
Q28Ch 30 · Health Assessment

In what sequence should a nurse perform physical assessment of the ABDOMEN?

  • A.Inspection → Percussion → Palpation → Auscultation
    ✗ Palpating/percussing before auscultating can artificially stimulate or suppress bowel sounds, giving a false baseline.
  • B.Inspection → Auscultation → Percussion → Palpation
    ✓ Correct. The abdomen is the EXCEPTION: auscultate before palpating/percussing to get accurate bowel sounds unaltered by manipulation.
  • C.Auscultation → Inspection → Palpation → Percussion
    ✗ Auscultation before inspection skips the visual assessment that should guide the entire examination.

Q29Ch 31 · Medication Administration

A nurse infusing IV vancomycin notes the site is red, swollen, and warm, and the patient reports pain. What should the nurse do FIRST?

  • A.Slow the infusion rate and apply a warm compress
    ✗ Slowing the rate does not address tissue damage and continues delivering a vesicant to a compromised site.
  • B.Stop the infusion and remove the peripheral IV catheter immediately
    ✓ Correct. Signs of phlebitis or infiltration require immediately stopping the infusion and removing the catheter — before any other intervention.
  • C.Flush the IV line with normal saline to assess patency
    ✗ Flushing pushes medication into already damaged or infiltrated tissue, worsening injury — especially dangerous with vancomycin.
  • D.Notify the physician before taking any action
    ✗ The nurse must stop the infusion and remove the catheter first; provider notification follows after the immediate threat is addressed.
Q30Ch 31 · Medication Administration

A nurse finds the patient's allergy list includes the prescribed drug class and cannot reach the prescribing physician. What is the APPROPRIATE action?

  • A.Administer the medication and document the notification attempt
    ✗ Administering a potentially allergenic drug could cause a life-threatening reaction; the medication must be held.
  • B.Hold the medication and contact the charge nurse or supervisor for guidance
    ✓ Correct. Patient safety requires holding the medication and escalating through the chain of command when a documented allergy conflict exists.
  • C.Ask another nurse to confirm the allergy and then give the medication
    ✗ Another nurse's confirmation does not change the documented allergy or make the drug safe to administer.
  • D.Give the medication since a physician ordered it
    ✗ A physician's order does not override a documented drug allergy; the nurse has an independent duty to ensure medication safety.
Unit 6 — Psychosocial Basis for Nursing Practice (Ch 33–37 · Q31–34)

A mastectomy patient refuses to look at the surgical site and says "I don't feel like a woman anymore." Which nursing diagnosis is MOST appropriate?

Q32Ch 35 · Spiritual Health

A patient nearing end of life says "I can't die yet — my family still needs me." Which response BEST addresses the patient's spiritual distress?

  • A."Your family will be well taken care of. Don't worry."
    ✗ This is false reassurance — it dismisses the patient's existential concern and shuts down the conversation.
  • B."Tell me more about what gives your life meaning and what your family needs from you."
    ✓ Correct. Spiritual care begins with exploring sources of meaning and purpose — open-ended exploration before any referral or reassurance.
  • C."Would you like me to call the hospital chaplain to speak with you?"
    ✗ A chaplain referral may be appropriate later, but the nurse should first explore the patient's spiritual needs directly.

Q33Ch 36 · Loss and Grief

A patient whose spouse died 5 months ago still cries daily and says "I can't imagine being happy again." The nurse BEST interprets this as:

  • A.Complicated grief — the patient needs immediate psychiatric referral
    ✗ Complicated (prolonged) grief involves severe functional impairment persisting beyond 12 months; intense sadness at 5 months is within normal range.
  • B.Normal grieving within the expected timeline for spousal bereavement
    ✓ Correct. Grief following spousal loss can involve intense sadness for 1–2 years or longer. Daily crying at 5 months is within the normal range.
  • C.Major depressive disorder requiring antidepressant therapy
    ✗ Diagnosing MDD at 5 months post-loss requires more than crying and anticipated sadness; clinical assessment must rule out normal grief first.
  • D.Denial — the first stage of Kübler-Ross's model
    ✗ Denial is the initial stage; this patient is clearly experiencing active grief, not refusing to accept the loss.
Q34Ch 37 · Stress and Coping

A patient admitted for hypertensive urgency says "I've been working 70-hour weeks since my company threatened layoffs." The nurse identifies this as which type of stressor?

  • A.Maturational (developmental) stressor
    ✗ Maturational stressors are tied to normal life-stage transitions (marriage, parenthood, retirement) — not external work threats.
  • B.Physiological stressor
    ✗ Physiological stressors arise from physical sources such as injury or disease — not workplace circumstances.
  • C.Situational (external) stressor
    ✓ Correct. Situational stressors arise from specific external life events or circumstances — the job threat and overwork are classic situational stressors.
  • D.Intrapsychic stressor
    ✗ Intrapsychic stressors originate from internal conflicts (values, beliefs, thoughts) rather than external circumstances.
Unit 7 — Physiological Basis for Nursing Practice (Ch 38–50 · Q35–50)
Q35Ch 38 · Activity and Exercise

A patient has left-sided weakness and is partially weight-bearing. What is the CORRECT wheelchair transfer technique?

  • A.Position the wheelchair on the patient's left (weak) side for easy pivot
    ✗ Positioning the chair on the weak side forces weight-bearing on the weak extremity, increasing fall risk.
  • B.Position the wheelchair on the right (strong) side and lead with the strong leg
    ✓ Correct. Transfer toward strength: wheelchair on the strong side, lead with the strong leg, pivot using intact strength.
  • C.Force weight-bearing on the left side to strengthen it during the transfer
    ✗ Forcing weight on a weak limb during transfer is unsafe and may cause injury; strengthening is done during therapy, not transfers.
  • D.Place the transfer belt around the patient's shoulders for support
    ✗ Transfer/gait belts are placed at the waist — never around the shoulders, which could cause cervical or shoulder injury.
Q36Ch 39 · Immobility

A patient on bed rest for 6 days reports calf pain with erythema and warmth over the posterior lower leg. This MOST likely represents:

  • A.Muscle atrophy from disuse
    ✗ Disuse atrophy causes weakness and reduced muscle bulk — not localized heat, redness, and pain.
  • B.Deep vein thrombosis (DVT)
    ✓ Correct. Calf pain, erythema, and warmth after prolonged immobility are classic DVT signs. Do NOT massage — it can dislodge the clot.
  • C.Peripheral arterial disease
    ✗ PAD causes cold, pale extremities and claudication (pain with activity) — not localized warmth and redness at rest.
  • D.Orthostatic hypotension
    ✗ Orthostatic hypotension causes dizziness upon standing — not localized calf findings.
Q37Ch 40 · Hygiene

A nurse is providing oral care to an unconscious patient. Which position BEST prevents aspiration?

  • A.Supine with head of bed flat (0°)
    ✗ Supine flat is the highest-risk position for aspiration as secretions pool at the pharynx.
  • B.Semi-Fowler's with neck hyperextended
    ✗ Neck hyperextension opens the glottis and increases aspiration risk by directing fluid toward the trachea.
  • C.Side-lying (lateral) position
    ✓ Correct. Side-lying allows oral secretions and rinse water to drain by gravity, preventing aspiration in unconscious patients.
  • D.High Fowler's at 90° without suction equipment present
    ✗ High Fowler's may help conscious patients but does not prevent aspiration in unconscious patients; suction must always be at the bedside.
Q38Ch 41 · Oxygenation

A COPD patient has SpO2 87% and is in moderate respiratory distress. What should the nurse do FIRST?

  • A.Prepare the patient for intubation
    ✗ Intubation is an advanced, invasive intervention; less invasive positioning and oxygen must be tried first.
  • B.Notify the physician immediately
    ✗ The nurse must take immediate independent actions (positioning, O₂) before calling the provider.
  • C.Position the patient upright (high Fowler's) and apply supplemental oxygen
    ✓ Correct. Upright positioning maximizes diaphragmatic excursion; oxygen corrects hypoxemia. These are immediate independent nursing actions that do not require an order for urgent application.
  • D.Administer the ordered PRN bronchodilator first
    ✗ Bronchodilator therapy is appropriate but comes after positioning and oxygen are initiated.
Q39Ch 41 · OxygenationSELECT ALL THAT APPLY

Which of the following actions reflect CORRECT airway suctioning technique? (Select all that apply)

  • A.Pre-oxygenate the patient with 100% oxygen for 30 seconds before each pass
    ✓ Pre-oxygenation minimizes the hypoxia that results from removing oxygen along with secretions.
  • B.Apply continuous suction while inserting the catheter
    ✗ Suction is applied only during WITHDRAWAL — not insertion. Applying it during advancement causes mucosal trauma and increases hypoxia.
  • C.Limit each pass to no longer than 10–15 seconds
    ✓ Limiting pass duration to 10–15 seconds prevents hypoxia and mucosal damage.
  • D.Maintain sterile technique throughout the procedure
    ✓ Sterile technique prevents introducing pathogens into the lower airway.
  • E.Suction the oropharynx (mouth) before the trachea to clear the most accessible area first
    ✗ The trachea is suctioned FIRST, then the oropharynx. Moving from clean (trachea) to contaminated (mouth) preserves sterile technique.
  • F.Allow the patient to rest 20–30 seconds between passes if needed
    ✓ Resting between passes allows SpO₂ to recover and reduces cumulative hypoxia risk.
Q40Ch 42 · Fluid & Electrolyte Balance

A patient is receiving 0.9% sodium chloride IV. The nurse correctly classifies this solution as:

  • A.Hypotonic — it will cause cells to swell
    ✗ Hypotonic solutions (0.45% NaCl, D5W after dextrose metabolism) cause water to move into cells. Normal saline does not.
  • B.Hypertonic — it will pull fluid from cells into the vasculature
    ✗ Hypertonic solutions (3% NaCl, D10W) shift water out of cells. 0.9% NaCl does not have this effect.
  • C.Isotonic — it has approximately the same osmolarity as plasma
    ✓ Correct. 0.9% NaCl (~308 mOsm/L) matches plasma osmolarity and causes no fluid shift between compartments.
  • D.Colloid — it contains large protein molecules
    ✗ Colloids contain large molecules such as albumin or blood products. 0.9% NaCl is a crystalloid solution.
Q41Ch 42 · Fluid & Electrolyte Balance

A heart failure patient has bilateral pitting edema in the lower extremities. The nurse recognizes this as:

  • A.Intracellular fluid deficit
    ✗ Intracellular fluid deficit causes cellular dehydration and is not visible as dependent edema.
  • B.Intravascular fluid deficit (hypovolemia)
    ✗ Hypovolemia causes tachycardia, hypotension, and decreased urine output — not pitting edema.
  • C.Extracellular (interstitial) fluid excess
    ✓ Correct. In heart failure, increased venous hydrostatic pressure shifts fluid from the vasculature into the interstitium, producing pitting edema.
  • D.Intracellular fluid excess
    ✗ Intracellular excess causes cellular swelling (seen in certain neurological emergencies) — not dependent pitting edema.
Q42Ch 43 · SleepSELECT ALL THAT APPLY

A patient has taken diphenhydramine (Benadryl) nightly for 6 months for insomnia. Which of the following are recognized risks of this practice? (Select all that apply)

  • A.Development of drug tolerance, requiring higher doses for the same effect
    ✓ Tolerance to diphenhydramine's sedative effect develops with regular use within days to weeks.
  • B.Improved sleep architecture and enhanced REM sleep quality
    ✗ Diphenhydramine actually suppresses REM sleep and disrupts normal sleep architecture with chronic use — it does not improve sleep quality.
  • C.Anticholinergic side effects including urinary retention and dry mouth
    ✓ Diphenhydramine's anticholinergic properties cause urinary retention, dry mouth, constipation, and blurred vision — especially problematic in older adults.
  • D.Next-day sedation and cognitive impairment ("hangover effect")
    ✓ Residual sedation and cognitive slowing the next day are well-recognized effects of diphenhydramine.
  • E.Increased fall risk, especially in older adults
    ✓ Sedation, dizziness, and anticholinergic confusion significantly increase fall risk in older adults.
  • F.Permanent cure of chronic insomnia with continued use
    ✗ Diphenhydramine does not cure insomnia; tolerance reduces its effectiveness over time and underlying causes remain untreated.
Q43Ch 44 · Pain Management

A post-op patient rates pain 9/10. Orders include acetaminophen 650 mg PO, ibuprofen 400 mg PO, and oxycodone 5 mg PO PRN. Which approach is MOST appropriate?

  • A.Acetaminophen alone — start with the lowest level
    ✗ Acetaminophen alone is appropriate for mild pain (1–3/10); it is insufficient for severe pain rated 9/10.
  • B.Oxycodone combined with acetaminophen for multimodal analgesia
    ✓ Correct. Per the WHO analgesic ladder, severe pain (7–10/10) warrants opioids. Combining with acetaminophen (multimodal) achieves better control with lower opioid doses.
  • C.Non-pharmacologic measures only (heat, cold, repositioning)
    ✗ Non-pharmacologic measures supplement but do not replace pharmacologic analgesia for severe post-operative pain.
  • D.Ibuprofen only — it treats post-surgical inflammation
    ✗ Ibuprofen alone addresses inflammation but is insufficient monotherapy for 9/10 post-operative pain.
Q44Ch 44 · Pain Management

A nurse needs to assess pain in a crying 4-year-old after a procedure. Which pain scale is MOST appropriate?

  • A.Numeric Rating Scale (NRS) 0–10
    ✗ NRS requires abstract numerical reasoning not fully developed in preschool-age children.
  • B.Visual Analog Scale (VAS)
    ✗ VAS requires abstract spatial reasoning; it is not developmentally appropriate for young children.
  • C.Wong-Baker FACES Pain Rating Scale
    ✓ Correct. FACES scale is validated for children ages 3+; children point to the face that matches their pain experience.
  • D.FLACC Scale (Face, Legs, Activity, Cry, Consolability)
    ✗ FLACC is for pre-verbal or cognitively impaired patients who cannot self-report; a 4-year-old can self-report and use FACES.
Q45Ch 45 · Nutrition

After inserting a nasogastric feeding tube, which method is the MOST reliable for confirming correct placement before initiating feedings?

  • A.Auscultating a "whoosh" sound over the epigastrium while injecting air
    ✗ The air auscultation method is unreliable and no longer recommended — sounds can be transmitted even from the esophagus or lung.
  • B.Observing that the patient is not coughing or showing respiratory distress
    ✗ Absence of respiratory symptoms does not rule out esophageal or pulmonary tube placement, especially in obtunded patients.
  • C.Checking the pH of gastric aspirate (≤ 4 indicates gastric placement)
    ✗ pH testing is a useful bedside check but can be unreliable with PPIs/H₂ blockers; it is not the gold standard for initial confirmation.
  • D.Obtaining an abdominal X-ray to confirm tube position
    ✓ Correct. Radiographic confirmation (X-ray) is the gold standard for verifying NG tube placement before initiating feedings.
Q46Ch 46 · Urinary Elimination

A patient has burning urination, frequency, and cloudy foul-smelling urine. The nurse suspects a UTI. What is the PRIORITY action?

  • A.Administer the ordered antibiotic immediately to begin treatment
    ✗ Starting antibiotics before obtaining culture eliminates the ability to identify the causative organism; targeted therapy becomes impossible.
  • B.Collect a midstream clean-catch urine specimen for culture and sensitivity BEFORE starting antibiotics
    ✓ Correct. Culture must be collected before antibiotics to identify the organism and sensitivity — this guides appropriate treatment.
  • C.Encourage the patient to drink 3 liters of fluid to flush the bladder
    ✗ Fluid encouragement is helpful but does not take priority over obtaining the culture that will guide the choice of antibiotic.
  • D.Insert an indwelling catheter to monitor urine output accurately
    ✗ Catheterization is invasive, increases infection risk (CAUTI), and is not appropriate management for an uncomplicated UTI.
Q47Ch 46 · Urinary Elimination

During sterile catheter insertion, the nurse accidentally touches the catheter tip against the patient's thigh. What should the nurse do?

  • A.Wipe the tip with a sterile gauze pad and proceed
    ✗ There is no accepted technique to restore sterility to a contaminated catheter tip — wiping does not make it sterile.
  • B.Continue — the thigh was cleaned with betadine before the procedure
    ✗ Skin cleaned with antiseptic is not a sterile surface; contact with any non-sterile surface (including prepped skin) contaminates the catheter.
  • C.Discard the contaminated catheter and open a new sterile kit
    ✓ Correct. Any break in sterile technique requires stopping and using new sterile equipment; a contaminated catheter is the primary cause of CAUTI.
  • D.Document the contamination and wait for a supervisor before proceeding
    ✗ The nurse should independently open new sterile equipment; waiting for a supervisor is unnecessary and delays patient care.
Q48Ch 48 · Skin Integrity & Wound Care

A bedbound patient has a shallow open wound on the coccyx with a moist pink/red wound bed and no slough or eschar. How should the nurse document this?

  • A.Stage 1 pressure injury — non-blanchable erythema on intact skin
    ✗ Stage 1 has no break in skin — only non-blanchable redness on intact skin. This wound is already open.
  • B.Stage 2 pressure injury — partial-thickness skin loss with exposed dermis
    ✓ Correct. Stage 2 = partial-thickness loss with viable (pink/red) wound bed, moist; may present as shallow open wound or intact/ruptured blister. No slough/eschar.
  • C.Stage 3 pressure injury — full-thickness skin loss
    ✗ Stage 3 shows full-thickness loss with visible subcutaneous fat; this patient has only partial-thickness (shallow) loss.
  • D.Unstageable — wound bed obscured by slough
    ✗ Unstageable injuries are covered by slough or eschar obscuring depth assessment; this wound bed is clearly visible with no coverage.
Q49Ch 49 · Sensory Alterations

An older adult in a windowless hospital room becomes agitated at night, insisting it is daytime. The nurse recognizes this as:

  • A.Sundowning syndrome secondary to dementia
    ✗ Sundowning occurs specifically in dementia patients at dusk/evening. There is no indication of dementia here, and the trigger is environmental deprivation.
  • B.Sensory overload from hospital monitoring equipment
    ✗ Sensory overload presents with anxiety and overwhelm from excessive stimuli — not time disorientation from lack of natural cues.
  • C.Sensory deprivation leading to altered perception and confusion
    ✓ Correct. Lack of natural light, clocks, and meaningful stimulation causes sensory deprivation — producing disorientation and misperception in hospitalized patients.
  • D.Acute psychosis unrelated to the hospitalization
    ✗ The environmental cause (no windows, monotonous setting) clearly points to sensory deprivation rather than a psychiatric origin.
Q50Ch 50 · Perioperative Nursing

A pre-op patient says "I've never had surgery before and I'm terrified I won't wake up." Which response is MOST therapeutic?

  • A."This is a very routine procedure — there's nothing to worry about."
    ✗ Minimizing fear with "nothing to worry about" invalidates the patient's concern and closes therapeutic communication.
  • B."Your surgeon is excellent and has performed this hundreds of times."
    ✗ Praising the surgeon deflects from the patient's emotional need and does not directly address their expressed fear.
  • C."It's completely normal to feel frightened. Tell me more about what concerns you."
    ✓ Correct. Acknowledging the emotion as normal and inviting elaboration validates the experience and opens therapeutic dialogue — the most therapeutic first response.
  • D."Would you like me to call the anesthesiologist to explain the risks?"
    ✗ Offering a referral before fully exploring the patient's concerns bypasses the nurse-patient relationship and may feel dismissive.
