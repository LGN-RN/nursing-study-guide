Ch 1 — Nursing Today
Question 1
Florence Nightingale is credited with which contribution to modern nursing?
A. Developing the first nursing diagnosis taxonomy
Incorrect. NANDA-I developed the nursing diagnosis taxonomy beginning in 1973 — long after Nightingale's era.
B. Establishing environmental principles to reduce infection and promote healing
Correct. Nightingale's Environmental Theory identified clean air, clean water, light, warmth, and hygiene as essential to patient recovery — principles foundational to modern nursing.
C. Writing the first Evidence-Based Practice clinical guidelines
Incorrect. EBP emerged formally in the 1990s. Although Nightingale used mortality data, formal EBP guidelines are a modern concept.
D. Creating the first nursing licensing examination
Incorrect. Nursing licensure laws in the U.S. began in the early 1900s — decades after Nightingale's contributions.
Ch 1 — Nursing Today
Question 2
The nursing scope of practice is BEST described as which of the following?
A. Performing tasks delegated by the physician
Incorrect. Nursing is an autonomous profession with independent functions — scope is not limited to physician-delegated tasks.
B. Activities a nurse is permitted to perform based on education, licensure, and competency
Correct. Scope of practice is defined by state Nurse Practice Acts and reflects what a nurse is educated, licensed, and competent to perform.
C. Following a facility's policy manual exclusively
Incorrect. Facility policies must align with the Nurse Practice Act, but scope of practice is defined at the state level — not by institutions.
D. Any health-related activity requested by the patient
Incorrect. Patient requests do not define scope of practice. Nurses may decline tasks outside their training, licensure, or ethical standards.
Ch 2 — Healthcare Delivery System
Question 3
A patient without health insurance presents to the emergency department in distress. By federal law, the hospital must:
A. Provide treatment only if pre-payment is arranged
Incorrect. Federal law prohibits hospitals from requiring payment before stabilizing care — this is a violation of EMTALA.
B. Perform a medical screening examination and provide stabilizing treatment
Correct. EMTALA (Emergency Medical Treatment and Labor Act) requires all Medicare-accepting hospitals to screen and stabilize any patient regardless of ability to pay.
C. Refer the patient to a community health center
Incorrect. Referral without a screening exam violates EMTALA. The patient must first be screened and stabilized.
D. Transfer the patient to a public hospital immediately
Incorrect. Transfer before stabilization is only permitted in specific circumstances (patient request, higher level of care needed) — not simply due to insurance status.

Question 4
Which healthcare financing model MOST emphasizes prevention and wellness over acute care treatment?

Incorrect. Fee-for-service pays providers for each service rendered — it incentivizes volume of care, not prevention.

Correct. Managed care organizations (HMOs, PPOs) incentivize preventive care and coordination to reduce costly acute interventions.

Incorrect. Indemnity insurance reimburses patients for any provider's services — no emphasis on prevention or care coordination.

Incorrect. Retrospective payment reimburses after services are delivered — it does not drive preventive care.

Ch 4 — Theoretical Foundations
Question 7
According to Maslow's hierarchy of needs, which nursing intervention has the HIGHEST priority?
A. Providing emotional support after a difficult diagnosis
Incorrect. Emotional support addresses a psychosocial need (love/belonging or esteem) — lower priority than physiological needs in Maslow's hierarchy.
B. Administering prescribed oxygen therapy to a hypoxic patient
Correct. Physiological needs — especially oxygenation — are always the highest priority. ABCs (Airway, Breathing, Circulation) take precedence over all other needs.
C. Encouraging socialization with other patients
Incorrect. Socialization addresses social/belonging needs — a mid-level need on Maslow's hierarchy, below physiological and safety needs.
D. Helping the patient define personal goals for recovery
Incorrect. Goal-setting relates to self-actualization — the highest level of Maslow's hierarchy, addressed only after lower needs are met.

Ch 5 — Evidence-Based Practice
Question 10
A nurse finds two research studies with conflicting results about a wound care intervention. The BEST next step in applying EBP is to:
A. Continue with current practice until a manager decides which study to follow
Incorrect. Defaulting to managerial opinion rather than systematically evaluating evidence is not consistent with EBP principles.
B. Search for a systematic review or meta-analysis on the topic
Correct. Systematic reviews and meta-analyses synthesize findings from multiple studies — they represent the highest level of evidence and help resolve conflicting individual results.

Incorrect. This biomedical view is explicitly excluded from the WHO definition, which encompasses much more than the absence of illness.
B. A state of complete physical, mental, and social well-being — not merely the absence of disease
Correct. WHO (1948) defined health holistically — physical, mental, and social well-being are all required components, not just freedom from disease.

Incorrect. Functional independence is one dimension of health but does not capture the full WHO definition, which includes mental and social well-being.

Ch 6 — Health and Wellness
Question 12
A nurse teaches a post-MI patient how to perform cardiac rehabilitation exercises. This BEST represents which level of prevention?
A. Primary prevention
Incorrect. Primary prevention stops disease before it occurs (e.g., immunizations, smoking cessation in healthy populations) — not post-disease intervention.
B. Secondary prevention
Correct. Secondary prevention limits the progression and consequences of existing disease — cardiac rehab after MI reduces future events and improves functional recovery.
C. Tertiary prevention
Incorrect. Tertiary prevention maximizes function and quality of life after significant disability is established — rehabilitation after a disabling stroke, for example.
D. Health promotion
Incorrect. Health promotion enhances well-being in healthy individuals — it is distinct from preventive interventions targeting existing disease risk.

According to Watson's Theory of Human Caring, the CENTRAL element of professional nursing practice is:

B. Transpersonal caring relationships
Correct. Watson's Human Caring Theory identifies the transpersonal caring relationship as the foundation of nursing — a deep human connection that supports healing beyond physical treatment.
C. Strict adherence to evidence-based medical protocols
Incorrect. Following protocols is necessary but insufficient — Watson's theory calls for nurses to bring themselves fully into a caring relationship with each patient.
D. Patient satisfaction scores and measurable outcome metrics
Incorrect. Patient satisfaction metrics are a modern administrative concept — Watson's theory focuses on the lived experience of caring, not quantitative measures.

Ch 13 — Nursing Diagnosis
Question 25
Which of the following is a correctly written nursing diagnosis in PES format?
A. "Risk for pneumonia due to hospitalization"
Incorrect. Risk diagnoses do not use "as evidenced by" (the problem hasn't occurred yet). Also, "due to" is not standard PES language — use "related to."
B. "Ineffective Airway Clearance related to excessive secretions as evidenced by audible gurgling and respiratory rate of 24"
Correct. This follows PES format: NANDA Problem label + related to (etiology) + as evidenced by (signs/symptoms). Specific, measurable, and nursing-focused.
C. "Bacterial pneumonia secondary to aspiration"
Incorrect. This is a medical diagnosis — nursing diagnoses describe the patient's human response to health conditions, not the pathological condition itself.
D. "Patient requires suctioning every 2 hours"
Incorrect. This is a nursing intervention, not a nursing diagnosis. Diagnoses identify the patient's health problem; interventions address it.

A nurse is writing a care plan for a patient at high risk for pressure injuries. Which interventions are appropriate to include? SELECT ALL THAT APPLY.
A. Reposition the patient at least every 2 hours
Correct. Scheduled repositioning relieves pressure on bony prominences, restoring blood flow and preventing ischemic tissue damage.

Correct. Moisture management prevents skin breakdown from urine and feces, which significantly increases pressure injury risk.

Incorrect. Massaging reddened bony prominences is contraindicated — it causes additional tissue trauma and worsens ischemia rather than improving circulation.

Correct. Malnutrition — particularly protein deficiency — significantly impairs skin integrity and healing. Nutritional optimization is essential pressure injury prevention.
